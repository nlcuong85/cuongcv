# Tone Profile

This file teaches the local AI agent how the candidate should sound. It should be based on the candidate's own emails, previous cover letters, project notes, or interview notes.

## Voice Rules

- Write directly and concretely.
- Sound modest, but not passive.
- Use the candidate's real seniority level.
- Prefer practical contribution over exaggerated enthusiasm.
- Mention the actual work in the job description, not only the job title.
- Use evidence-backed sentences instead of broad personality claims.

## Sentence Pattern

Preferred:

- I am interested in this role because the work is concrete: <specific job task>, <specific job task>, and <specific job task>.
- In <project or role>, I worked on <evidence>, which connects well to <job requirement>.
- I can contribute most strongly where <candidate strength> needs to become <employer value>.

Avoid:

- I am highly motivated and passionate about your company.
- I believe I am the perfect candidate.
- I have extensive experience in every required area.
- Your company is a global leader and therefore very attractive to me.

## Cover Letter Tone

- Paragraph 1 should explain why the job work itself fits.
- Paragraph 2 should show the strongest evidence.
- Paragraph 3 should show a second different proof point.
- Paragraph 4 should explain company or role motivation without generic praise.
- Paragraph 5 should close practically with availability, documents, or caveats.

## CV Tone

- Overview should be compact and evidence-backed.
- Skills should be factual, not inflated.
- If a job asks for a skill the candidate does not have, mention it only as a review risk, not as a claimed skill.
